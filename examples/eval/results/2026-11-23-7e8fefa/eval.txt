VERDICT
  Keyword search alone finds the right memory 65% of the time and injects junk on 17% of unanswerable questions.
  The MCP lookup tool finds it 65% of the time in its top 5 and returns something on 50% of unanswerable questions.
  'always' raises that to 80% (+15 points) and answers in about 5.8 ms, but keeps a helper process running (about 610 MB of memory).
  'on_miss' reaches 80% (+15 points, 100% of that gain) and loads the model on only 54% of questions, with no helper process, but each of those takes about 2.4 s.
  Pick 'always' if you can spare the memory and want the best results; 'on_miss' if you cannot, or want nothing running in the background.
  Meaning search is better at staying quiet when there is no answer (0% junk vs 17%).
  The weak-match gate cuts junk from 50% to 17% of unanswerable questions without losing a found memory.

Small sample (26 questions): a one-question change moves a score by about 5 points.

LIMITS
  - A question counts as found if ANY message from an expected session comes back.
  - No current-project boost is applied, so every project is treated the same.
  - A rival session that also answers a question counts against the method unless it is listed in 'expect'.
  - Every question here is phrased about the past, which trips the on_miss gate more often than everyday prompts do; remember_baseline.py shows the rate on your real prompts.
  - Meaning search runs here with the model already loaded; a cold start adds about 2.4 s per process.
  - mcp is the search_transcripts tool as Claude calls it: up to 20 rows, no weak-match gate, no recency weighting and no machine-message filter; its 'injected' count is rows returned.
  - Keyword recency is measured from a fixed time (2026-06-01T00:00:00Z), not the clock.
